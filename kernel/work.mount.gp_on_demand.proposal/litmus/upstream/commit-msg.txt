Documentation/litmus-tests: add the mount reference count tests

The reference count of a mount is split into per-CPU gets and puts and
not everybody who touches it takes mount_lock. A holder drops a
reference on the fast path of mntput_no_expire() under rcu_read_lock()
as long as it sees ->mnt_ns non-NULL. A walker in __legitimize_mnt()
takes one speculatively during an RCU path walk and drops it again
under mount_lock when the seqcount has changed. And the unmounter sums
the counters under mount_lock with mnt_get_count() and finishes the
mount off without a grace period when the sum says nothing else holds
it.

That rests on three barriers and one grace period: the smp_wmb()
before the fast-path put, the smp_mb() between the increment and the
re-read of the seqcount in __legitimize_mnt(), the smp_mb() between
the puts pass and the gets pass of mnt_get_count(), and the
synchronize_rcu_expedited() that mntput_unmounted() waits for when the
sum came out at more than one.

Add the litmus tests that make that argument in the kernel's memory
model. Each is a two-process cut of fs/namespace.c with the per-CPU
counters as one variable per CPU, write_seqlock() spelled out as a
spinlock, a seqcount store and smp_wmb(), and read_seqretry() as
smp_rmb() and READ_ONCE(). The tests of the tree's shape are "Never".
Next to them sit mutations with one ingredient removed, the holder's
smp_wmb(), the inner smp_mb() of mnt_get_count() or the order of its
two passes, the walker's smp_mb(), the lock around the walker's look
at ->mnt_flags, the grace period, and each of those is "Sometimes".
That shows which barrier carries which pairing.

Two changes to fs/namespace.c lean on this. The first is finishing an
unmounted mount off without a grace period when the count under
mount_lock is one: a holder's get is counted whenever its put is, and
a walker's increment is either counted or the walker sees the seqcount
change and drops under the lock, so the one reference the sum found is
the mount's own. The second is dropping the smp_mb() between
lock_mount_hash() and the sum in mntput_no_expire_slowpath() and
do_umount(): the smp_mb() inside mnt_get_count() sits after the
seqcount store and before the gets loads in program order, which is
the full barrier the store buffering pairing with the walker needs, so
the outer one ordered nothing the inner one does not.

The tests run with herd7 against tools/memory-model:

    cd tools/memory-model
    scripts/checklitmus.sh ../../Documentation/litmus-tests/fs/<test>

The memory model is the contract that every architecture's smp_mb(),
smp_wmb(), READ_ONCE(), WRITE_ONCE(), spinlock and RCU implementation
has to satisfy. A "Never" there holds on every architecture the kernel
runs on.
