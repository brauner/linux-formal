# TLA+ model of work.mount.gp_on_demand.proposal

Tree: work.mount.gp_on_demand.proposal at 4379d47fd314 "ipc: unmount the
mqueue mount with kern_unmount()", on vfs-7.4.mount c947dc6ab4f4 (split
per-CPU get/put counters summed puts first with the smp_mb() of
mnt_get_count(), the smp_wmb() before the fast-path decrement of
mntput_no_expire(), the covers design where nothing stays attached to an
unmounted mount).  The fs/namespace.c patches of the series, in order:

* 3f208426f43d "fs: split the final put out of mntput_no_expire_slowpath()"
  (NFC: mntput_final_locked(), mntput_queue_cleanup())
* fa7ea944eb74 "fs: drop the caller's umount(2) reference under mount_lock"
  (do_umount() drops the caller's reference in the write section right
  after umount_tree(); it is never the last one)
* 754381fa6d6c "fs: unmount without a grace period when nothing else holds
  the mounts" (mntput_unheld(), mntput_unmounted(); namespace_unlock()
  calls mntput_unmounted(&head))
* 6b01a3707d69 "fs: kern_unmount() without a grace period for an unheld
  mount" (kern_unmount_array(): mnt_make_shortterm() then mntput_unmounted())
* 2775643beb95 (selftest), 4379d47fd314 (free_ipc_ns() -> kern_unmount())

The five RCU-delayed-free conversions below them (unicode, ntfs3 x2, ovl,
ceph) are not modelled.  Line numbers below are fs/namespace.c at
4379d47fd314.

This directory follows `kernel/work.mount.gp_on_demand/` (one mount, the
first shapes of the series) and the conventions of `kernel/mount/`.  The
delta: a batch of TWO mounts unmounted in one write section,
mntput_unmounted()'s one-lock-hold peek over the batch, the kern_unmount()
feeder, three unmount modes, and the mutations of this shape.  The
historical `GP_ON_DEMAND` task-work shape and the rejected `OWN_DROP` of
the old model are dropped: each would need its own umounter state machine
over the batch, and the old directory keeps them.

## The batch and the tasks

Two mounts, A and B.  `BatchOrder` (`<<"A","B">>` or `<<"B","A">>`) is
the order mntput_unmounted() walks the list `head`; umount_tree() filled
`unmounted` with hlist_add_head() (L1953), so it processed the mounts in
the opposite order, and the model pushes umount_tree()'s stores in that
opposite order (`Rev`).  A is `Root`, the mount umount(2) was called on;
it carries the caller's reference in MODE "umount".

| Task | Kernel |
|------|--------|
| `U` | the umounter: do_umount() + namespace_unlock(), put_mnt_ns(), or kern_unmount_array() |
| `WA`, `WB` | an RCU path walker of A or B: read_seqbegin(mount_lock), __lookup_mnt(), __legitimize_mnt(), use, mntput() |
| `HA`, `HB` | a holder of A or B: a reference from the start (an open file, fs->pwd), mntget()/mntput() pairs, migration, the final mntput() |

Layouts (the cfg prefix): `hw` (HA, WB), `wh` (WA, HB), `hh`, `ww`, `h`
(HA only: nothing touches B), `w` (WA only), `hwa` (HA and WA: the walker
can reach A as the holder's fs->pwd, read under fs->seq with no reference
of its own; the holder's chdir() can drop the reference any time), `full`
(all four).  A kern_mount() is never in a namespace and no RCU walker
finds it, so the "kern" configurations have holders only.

Modes (`MODE`):

| Mode | Kernel |
|------|--------|
| `"umount"` | umount(2) of A whose umount_tree() takes B along (a lazy umount of the tree, or a synchronous umount with B the propagated copy that propagate_mount_busy() checks at a count of one); do_umount() drops the caller's reference in the write section; namespace_unlock() -> mntput_unmounted() |
| `"nsdeath"` | put_mnt_ns(): umount_tree(ns->root, 0), no caller's reference, no MNT_SYNC_UMOUNT, then namespace_unlock() -> mntput_unmounted() |
| `"kern"` | kern_unmount_array(): mnt_make_shortterm() stores mnt_ns = NULL with no lock and no seqcount bump, hlist_add_head(), mntput_unmounted() |

The memory model is the one of `kernel/mount/tla/MntPut.tla`: per-CPU FIFO
store buffers with store forwarding, drained by smp_mb(), by the RMW of
spin_lock(), by a migration and by a grace period; `WEAK_STORES` lets a
CPU's stores to different words retire out of order unless an smp_wmb()
sits between them.  A grace period ends only when every task that was in
an RCU read section when it started has left it.

## Switches

Every configuration runs with `FIX_SPLIT_COUNT` and `FIX_PUT_WMB` on
unless it says so (the tree the series sits on), and with the mainline
pieces `FIX_MB_LEGIT`, `FIX_MB_UMOUNT`, `FIX_MB_PUT`, `FIX_RCU_DELAY`,
`FIX_PUT_RCU`, `FIX_SYNC_FLAG`, `FIX_DOOMED_FLAG` on (documented in
`kernel/mount/tla/README.md`).

| Constant | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| `MODE`, `LAZY` | see above; `LAZY` is umount2(MNT_DETACH) in MODE "umount" |
| `BatchOrder` | the list order of the peek |
| `FIX_MB_PEEK` | off: no smp_mb() in mntput_unmounted() after lock_mount_hash() |
| `FIX_RCU_DELAY` | off: no synchronize_rcu_expedited() before the puts of the held mounts |
| `PEEK_LOOSE` | mntput_unheld() finishes a mount off at a count of two as well |
| `PREV_SHAPE` | the shape of d5546255be07: one lock hold per mount, plain puts after the first held one |
| `QUEUE_LATE` | the cleanups are queued after the grace period and the held puts (the task-work timing of a user task) instead of right after the peek (the MNT_INTERNAL timing) |
| `FIX_PUT_RECHECK` | exploratory: the fast path re-reads mnt_ns after its decrement, see above; meant to run with `FIX_RCU_DELAY` off |
| `GetBudget`, `MIGRATE`, `MigBudget`, `NCPU`, `WEAK_STORES`, `FIX_SPLIT_COUNT`, `SPLIT_GETS_FIRST` | as in `kernel/mount/`; `MigBudget` is 2 when `MIGRATE` is on (the torn sum needs the holder to get on one CPU and put on the other, after the sum passed the first) |

## What is checked

| Invariant | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| `TypeOK` | types; the finished mounts are a subset of `head`, `head` and `held` are disjoint |
| `Ledger` | each mount's counters, buffered stores included, add up to its own reference plus the references the tasks hold on it |
| `NoUAF` | nobody touches a freed mount: no walker or holder of it at a label that reads or writes it, and U does not have it on `head` or `held` or as the mount it is summing or putting |
| `DoomedIsLast` | MNT_DOOMED means the own reference is gone and nobody but a walker in its transient window holds one |
| `NoNegative` | mnt_get_count() never comes out negative under mount_lock (the WARN_ON of the slow path) |
| `SyncClean` | a synchronous umount that returned 0 left no reference behind, and both cleanups ran from the caller: the filesystems are shut down before umount(2) returns (the selftest's claim) |
| `Freed`, `AllDone` | both mounts are freed unless the umount was refused; every task finishes |

Witnesses (expected to fail): `NoFastFinal`, the peek never drops an own
reference (its violation shows a mount finished without any grace
period); `NoFinishAfterHeld`, the peek never drops an own reference once
an earlier mount of the batch went on `held` (its violation is the point
of the one-lock-hold restructure; with `PREV_SHAPE` it holds, that shape
put every mount after the first held one the plain way after the grace
period).

## What the model says about the code

* The safety argument of 754381fa6d6c holds in every layout, order and
  mode: a count of exactly one under mount_lock is the mount's own
  reference.  The split counters (`h_sync_torn_sum`), the walker's
  barrier and the slow path's barrier (`hwa_lazy_no_mb_legit`,
  `hwa_lazy_no_mb_put`), the MNT_DOOMED test (`w_lazy_no_doomed_flag`)
  and the grace period for every other count (`*_no_rcu_delay`) are each
  load-bearing.  The peek must be the last put of an unheld mount, as the
  old model found (its OWN_DROP); here the caller's reference goes in the
  write section and the own one at the peek.
* The restructure is what it says: an unheld mount behind a held one is
  finished without a grace period (`*_witness_after_held`), the previous
  shape did not (`h_prev_witness_after_held`), and both shapes are safe.
* The smp_mb() of mntput_unmounted() (L1542) has no partner this
  abstraction can show, for three reasons that are each a fact about the
  code: a mount whose count is one has no holder, so no walker can reach
  it as a pwd (the hash is gone since the write section); a holder that
  drops its reference after the write section reads mnt_ns NULL and takes
  the slow path, whose lock_mount_hash() bumps the seqcount and
  invalidates any walker that sampled it after the unmount; and a kern
  mount has no walkers.  What the barrier is for is a weakly ordered
  machine where a holder still reads a stale non-NULL mnt_ns after the
  unlock and decrements on the fast path with no seqcount bump, while a
  walker that found the mount as that holder's pwd legitimizes against
  the umounter's seqcount store: a Dekker between the umounter's store
  and loads and the walker's store and load.  The single-visible-state
  store-buffer abstraction (every flushed store is seen by everybody at
  once, every reader sees the latest flushed value) cannot express the
  stale read, so the `*_no_mb_peek` configurations pass.  Keep the
  barrier.  The comment's "see mntput_no_expire()" is the mnt_get_count()
  pairing (puts first, smp_mb(), gets, against the smp_wmb() of the fast
  path), not a second Dekker: the fast path has no full barrier of its
  own, and the model finds the holder side safe with or without L1542.
* do_umount()'s drop of the caller's reference (L2097) is a plain
  mnt_dec_count() without the smp_wmb() of mntput_no_expire(): fine, it
  runs under mount_lock on the task that peeks next and is read back
  under the same lock; the model stores it plain.
* mntput_queue_cleanup() queues the cleanups as task work for a user
  task, so they run after mntput_unmounted() returns, i.e. after the
  grace period and the held puts; for MNT_INTERNAL mounts (every
  kern_mount()) cleanup_mnt() runs right there.  The model starts the RCU
  delay at the earliest point (`UQueue` right after the peek) and
  `QUEUE_LATE` at the latest; both pass.

## Not modelled

* Only two mounts, one walker and one holder per mount, two CPUs, one
  extra mntget()/mntput() pair per holder, two migrations.  `full_lazy` is
  the biggest run.
* Path walks reach an unmounted mount only through __lookup_mnt() (gone
  after the write section) or a holder's fs->pwd; mnt_parent walks from a
  child still attached (there are none after umount_tree() with the
  covers design), covers, mnt_pins, expiry lists, shrink_dentry_list()
  and the dentry side are not modelled.
* The sync batch stands for a mount plus a propagated copy checked at a
  count of one; shrink_submounts() and the propagation algebra are the
  business of `kernel/mount/` and `kernel/work.mount.fixes.3/`.
* Stale reads on weakly ordered hardware (see above): the abstraction has
  one visible copy of memory; `WEAK_STORES` only reorders a CPU's own
  stores.
* The intermediate shape of this branch's reflog ("let the grace period
  for held mounts elapse during the cleanups", 1a29b81749f6 with
  get_state_synchronize_rcu() and a task-work mntput_held_work()) is
  gone from the series and is not modelled.
* MNT_INTERNAL versus task-work cleanup timing is covered only by the two
  extremes (`UQueue` placement, `QUEUE_LATE`).

### The two five-task runs

`full_lazy` and `full_lazy_g0` (holder and walker on both mounts) were
stopped by hand after 225M and 181M states generated (49.7M and 51.3M
